How does BlueView modular compare with traditional site-built construction? Here’s a quick look at the differences that matter.

BlueView galvanized steel-framed modular homes versus typical site-built homes
BlueView Modular HomesGalvanized Steel-Framed Construction Traditional Site-Built HomesTypical Construction Methods
Noncombustible steel framing

Galvanized steel framing adds no fuel to a fire.
Wood framing is common

Wood is combustible. Fire-rated assemblies can still provide protection.
Framing that doesn’t rot

Steel does not absorb moisture or rot; galvanizing helps resist corrosion.
Wood needs moisture protection

Persistent moisture can lead to rot. Proper waterproofing and maintenance matter.
Earthquake resilient

Our buildings, foundations, and connections are engineered to resist earthquake forces based on your site’s conditions and applicable seismic requirements.
Seismic engineering is also required

Site-built homes must meet applicable seismic requirements. Design and installation determine performance.
Factory-built home & finishes

Modules and planned finishes are completed in a controlled factory environment.
Built mainly on your property

Structure and finishes are generally completed on site, with multiple trades working at the property.
Less construction on your lot

Site work focuses on foundations, utilities, installation, connections, and final work.
More construction on your lot

Most of the build happens at the property, with more on-site labor and coordination.
Save money on labor and time spent on site

Factory efficiency can reduce labor costs, waste, and time spent on site.
Costs can increase and delays can happen

Doing most of the work on site can cause delays, quality-control challenges, and higher costs.
